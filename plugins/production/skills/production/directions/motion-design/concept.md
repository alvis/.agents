# Concept

Read from `directions/motion-design.md` step 2, after the direction questions. Turn the answers into five story concepts for the board, each built on a proven recipe or story device, and keep the chosen one honest through the storyboard.

## Define the serving

Before choosing anything, write one sentence: "For ⟨audience⟩, create ⟨one learning outcome, feeling, experience, or useful action⟩ through ⟨form⟩ in ⟨duration⟩." For an explainer, name what the viewer can do afterwards; for a music video, the relationship between track and image; for promotion, add the next action. Not every piece needs a plot or a sales ask.

## Choose what drives the piece

Separate purpose (teach, entertain, express, inform, promote), structure (story, explanation, comparison, performance, association, cycle), and timing authority (the learning steps, the track, the spoken performance, or the delivery slot). Pick one primary driver; it selects the shelves to draw recipes from. The [recipe index](references/recipes.md) lists every recipe with its use-when line; open a shelf file only for recipes you draft.

| Primary driver | Shelves | Earns attention through | Success looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A character's goal or changing relationship | short (A), story (STO), comedy (COM) | Actions and consequences | An earned dramatic or emotional outcome |
| A question, concept, process, or skill | explainer (E), science (DIS), history (HIS) | Discovery and growing understanding | The viewer can explain, predict, or do something |
| A song, performance, or track | music video (V), sensory (MUS) | Musical form, expression, and recurrence | This music becomes a distinct experience through these images |
| Testimony, evidence, or an interpretation | essay (X1–X2), mystery (DOC) | A voice, reasoning, or a perspective | A supported understanding, evidence kept apart from interpretation |
| A poem, process, collection, or visual identity | essay (X3–X6), sensory (MUS) | Association, pattern, comparison, or sensory pleasure | The intended expression, satisfaction, or recognition |
| A product, service, or event | promo (R) | A useful promise or a demonstration | Viewers understand the offer and the next action |
| A person or an occasion | [story devices](references/story-devices.md), story (STO), comedy (COM) | Recognition and affection | The subject feels seen and the audience recognises them |

## Keep format, structure, and technique apart

Format is the experience viewers recognise (comic history, an investigation, a storytime, a performance). Structure is how it develops (cause and consequence, prediction and explanation, attempts and reversal, motif and return). Technique is how it looks and moves, chosen from the [style catalogue](references/styles.md). Changing the format needs a new hook, not just new rendering. Matches that usually work: limited 2D characters for dialogue and storytime; cut-out collage for archive-led history; maps and diagrams for causality; charts and simulations for comparisons; kinetic type for words; 3D cutaways for mechanisms; full-character 2D or 3D for performance; tactile or stop-motion looks for materials; mixed media for documentary; environmental loops for atmosphere.

## Draft five concepts

Draft five concepts, each on a different recipe or device, so the user chooses between ideas rather than variations of one; make at least one unexpected for the occasion. Draw them from at least two shelves when the driver allows, because one shelf's recipes share a structure.

- Fill the recipe's slots with the user's facts. Name observable actions, explanatory relationships, musical behaviour, or image associations rather than themes: "wants acceptance" still needs an action, "explains fractions" still needs a comparison.
- Borrow a recipe's order, never another work's characters, imagery, or ending.
- Use only facts the user gave; a slot with no fact stays a question for the user, not an invention.
- Give each concept a name, a one-line pitch, its recipe code, four to eight key moments, where the music hook lands, and its reward.

## Define the reward

State what the viewer should learn, feel, recognise, or enjoy, and build the opening around that promise with [hooks](directions/motion-design/hooks.md). A discovery builds to an answer, a narrative earns a consequence, a music video returns to its refrain, and an ambient loop sustains a state; do not force a climax onto a form whose value is continuity.

## Combine forms

Decide purpose, sequence, and which form sets the timing as three separate choices. The primary purpose governs and a second form only supports it, so name which requirement wins when they conflict:

- E8 education inside an A narrative: the lesson sets the progression and the story supplies only the character experience; never bend the principle to rescue the plot.
- An explanation with a teaching song: meaning and transfer govern, not recall alone; rewrite the rhyme before weakening the fact.
- V2 visual score with R8 promotion: keep both the music and the identity legible; choose a fitting excerpt and add no unrelated sales ending.
- X1 testimony with X3 visual poetry: the account's meaning and context govern; invented imagery never poses as documentary proof.
- X4 process with R5 demonstration: show the real transformation; never invent speed, precision, or results for a prettier loop.
- E7 data with an X2 essay: the evidence limits the conclusion; keep uncertainty visible whenever it changes the takeaway.

When two shelves cover the same subject, draft from the one whose driver matches the piece: A3 and STO1 both show care through a small task, A1 and COM2 both build comedy from repeated attempts, A5 and STO2 both reverse a judgement, E1 and DIS7 both ask for a prediction, E6 and DIS6 both change scale, and X4 and MUS6 both follow a satisfying process.

## Taste before production

Before the storyboard is approved, read the rough panels in order and speak or sing the words at their intended pace. Ask a narrative viewer what happened, a learner to apply the idea, and judge a music video against the actual track; [quality](directions/motion-design/quality.md) holds the finish test for each form. Recipe timings are starting allocations, not laws about attention; change them when the material needs a different rhythm.
