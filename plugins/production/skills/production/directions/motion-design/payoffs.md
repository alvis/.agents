# Payoffs, sharing and packaging

Read from `directions/motion-design/hooks.md` when writing the storyboard's payoff, catchphrases, title and cover.

## Payoffs

Rewards can arrive throughout: a lesson may answer early and spend its time on why; a music video rewards through performance and returning motifs; a loop offers continuity without a climax. A lesson's test is transfer: can the viewer use the idea on a new case? For a piece built toward a definite arrival:

| Payoff | Method | Finish test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comic reversal | Prediction → prepare its cause → a different result | Surprising, yet possible under the established rules? |
| Emotional reclassification | A gesture → a changed context → the gesture again | Does the same action now carry a different meaning? |
| Earned competence | Difficulty → visible learning → the challenge again, met differently | Is the new ability shown rather than asserted? |
| Product proof | A condition → the intervention → a comparable result | Can the viewer name what the product changed? |
| Satisfying completion | Teach a pattern → develop it → resolve the gap | Rewarding without tricking a replay? |
| Invitation | An appealing activity → room to take part → name the next action | Does the viewer know what joining would mean? |

A twist is optional. Add a second only when it deepens the payoff; a total reset erases the consequence. In cyclical or musical work an intentional return can be the central pleasure.

## Share reason

Complete one sentence for the intended audience. It is a hypothesis, not evidence that anyone will share.

- **Recognition**: "I'd send this to ⟨person⟩ because it captures ⟨situation we know⟩." Sharpen with a specific behaviour, not a broad label.
- **Useful explanation**: "This makes ⟨confusing thing⟩ easier to understand." The result stays accurate; the metaphor must not smuggle in a false demonstration.
- **Affection**: "This says ⟨what I want them to hear⟩ without my writing a speech." Let the relationship carry the feeling, not generic sentimental music.
- **Delight**: "You need to see what happens when ⟨clear setup⟩." The description keeps the surprise and honestly names its kind.
- **Participation**: "We could make our own version of ⟨simple pattern⟩." The pattern is adaptable without copying protected material.

Write the hook line and tagline as words a viewer could quote inside that sentence.

Finish test: remove any "like, comment and share" request; is something still worth passing on?

## Replay as a bonus

- **Fair clue**: a visible clue gains a second meaning after the ending; the first viewing is complete without it.
- **Rhythmic**: the last sound leads into the first; keep consequences when the piece promises a story, reset coherently when it promises a cycle.
- **Learning**: stable terms and landmarks make steps easy to revisit. Needing a replay to see an obscured essential step is a clarity defect.
- **Atmospheric**: a place or pattern worth inhabiting, with no pretended hidden reveal.
- **Layered**: secondary reactions or details that enrich a scene without hiding essential information there.

## Package the same promise

- **Title**: names the situation or question; never the twist that is the reward, never a verdict on the work.
- **Cover**: shows the real promise: goal and obstacle, a question or comparison, the performer or visual identity, or a characteristic scene. Never promise a puzzle when the piece offers a mood.
- **Caption**: supplies missing context, access or the next action; it cannot repair a broken story.
- **Series**: keep one recurring promise, relationship or formal rule; vary the obstacle, topic and outcome.
- **Trend**: use one only when its rhythm or convention improves this idea; if removing it leaves no premise, develop the premise first.
- **Cut-down**: rebuild a shorter version as a smaller complete sequence with its own local reward (one explained relation, a demonstration, a complete musical phrase, a comic beat, an honest sample of the atmosphere); never cut a musical phrase mid-thought to hit a limit.
