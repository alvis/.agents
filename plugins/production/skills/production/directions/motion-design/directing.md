# Directing

Read from `directions/motion-design.md` at step 2, and again at steps 4 and 7. Through every step of `motion-design`, act as a world-class animation director and story editor: the director decides what each moment shows and how the hero acts it; the story editor cuts every moment that does not move the viewer toward the message.

## Aim at the message

The storyboard's `message` is the one thing the viewer must be able to say in their own words when the piece ends. Write it per video from the user's answers before drafting concepts, as a sentence a viewer could repeat ("a pen is all it takes to start"), not a theme ("creativity"). Every scene's beat either sets up, turns, or pays off that sentence; a beat that does neither is cut or rewritten. The finish test in [quality](directions/motion-design/quality.md#quality-gate) checks that a fresh viewer can say it.

## Act through the hero

- Emotion lives in the eyes, posture, and timing, never in a caption. Show surprise as widened eyes and a held beat, doubt as a glance away and a slumped line of action, resolve as a lifted chin and a quicker tempo; on-screen text never names a feeling.
- Make the eyes expressive: give them a readable gaze direction, lids and brows that change shape with each feeling, and pupils that lead a head turn by a few frames, because the eyes are where a viewer looks first on a character.
- Keep the hero alive between beats with blinks and breathing. A hold without either reads as a frozen drawing; [build](directions/motion-design/build.md#motion-from-functions) gives the functions that drive them.
- Let timing carry the acting: a hold before a reaction, a fast take on surprise, a slow settle on relief. Choose a [character style](references/characters.md) whose eyes can carry this; a style with dot eyes moves the expression into posture and timing.

## Write every scene

For every scene write, in the [storyboard](templates/storyboard.md) scene keys:

- **SCENE n and its beat**: the scene number and the single story beat it delivers toward the message.
- **Frame**: the camera (shot size, angle, and move), the hero's position, the props, and the light.
- **Motion**: the anticipation that prepares the action, the action itself, and the follow-through that settles it.
- **Sound**: the cue that sells the action, such as a whoosh on the anticipation, a hit on the contact, or a sting on the reveal, placed on its beat in the music plan.

A scene missing any of the four is not ready for approval, because the builder would invent the missing choice.

## Product pieces

When the piece promotes a product, service, or event, gather four inputs during the [interview](directions/motion-design/interview.md), build every concept around them, and write them in the storyboard's product section:

- **Tagline**: the line the piece lands on, written with [hooks](directions/motion-design/hooks.md).
- **Facts**: the true, checkable claims the piece may show; never invent one.
- **Colour tone**: the brand palette or tone, if the user has one; it seeds the style sheet.
- **CTA**: the one next action the viewer takes.
