# Composition for explanation, music and text

Read from `directions/motion-design/composition.md` when a shot explains a system, follows the track's structure, or animates words.

## C9 · Overview, focus, connect, return
- Use when: explaining a system, a worked example, data, or a process.
- Ingredients: one explanatory question; a simple map or diagram; the relation in focus; stable labels; a whole-system return.
- Method: show the overview → pick out the relevant part → change only what demonstrates this step → connect its output to the next part → return to the overview, now understandable.
- Variations: keep a locator visible when scale or viewpoint changes; name unfamiliar parts before several interact; keep intermediate states the next step depends on.
- Fix: every sentence brings a new picture; keep one stable explanatory world and let changes show the relations.
- Finish test: can the viewer trace the mechanism and locate the current step without the narrator reorienting them?

## C10 · Accent, phrase, section
- Use when: a music video, animated performance, visual score or rhythmic promo.
- Ingredients: the actual track; local gestures; a recurring visual phrase; a plan for section-level variation.
- Method: choose a few accents worth syncing → let each musical phrase carry one complete action → let each section change the larger arrangement → keep a recognisable anchor across returns.
- Variations: a gesture can span several beats; a quiet section can reduce movement; a return need not be louder or larger.
- Fix: every beat triggers a cut and the video has no larger shape; give accents, phrases and sections different jobs.
- Finish test: does the picture reflect the track's phrasing, contrast and returning ideas, not only its pulse?

## C11 · Phrase, emphasis, space
- Use when: a lyric video, spoken word, educational keywords or kinetic type.
- Ingredients: approved text; its spoken or sung pace; the words that change the meaning; a readable hierarchy; breathing room.
- Method: group words by meaning → make the phrase readable → emphasise only words that change the interpretation → let motion express a linguistic relation → hold or clear the phrase when the thought moves on.
- Variations: a line can settle for reading first, then transform. Animated keywords are not captions; keep an access caption track where the platform needs one.
- Fix: constantly animated words compete with the voice or song; reduce simultaneous change and animate by phrase, not syllable.
- Finish test: can the intended viewer read at performance pace and see why each emphasis lands where it does?
