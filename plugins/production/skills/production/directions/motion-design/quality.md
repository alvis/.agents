# Quality review

Read from `directions/motion-design.md` when reviewing a storyboard, an animatic or a render.

Judge the piece by its own form: a lesson must be usable, a music video must work with its track, a poem may stay ambiguous, a loop may sustain a state. None needs a plot conflict to qualify. Fix the cause, not the decoration. Render faults (jumps, drift, nondeterminism) belong to the failure signals in [build](directions/motion-design/build.md); this file covers whether the film works.

## Quality gate

- **Clear for its purpose**: the intended viewer follows what must be understood. A tutorial's required action is legible; a poem may keep its ambiguity; a music video is judged with its music.
- **Specific**: the characters, observations, objects or benefit could not be swapped for a generic example.
- **Coherent in its form**: a narrative has causal continuity, an explanation sound reasoning, a music video an authored sound–image relation, an essay defensible connections, a poem intentional associations, a loop a coherent cycle.
- **Rewarding**: the opening promise from [hooks](directions/motion-design/hooks.md) is kept through an answer, consequence, performance, recognition or sustained experience.
- **Well paced**: important actions and reactions have room, repetition has a job, and shortening has not broken comprehension.
- **Integrated**: composition, music and language never compete for the same instant.
- **Credible**: mechanisms and diagrams are checked, testimony is distinguished from reconstruction, data supports the inference, a commercial demonstration matches the real offer, and reused work is credited with rights recorded through `track`.
- **Worth passing on**: a plausible, audience-specific share reason from [payoffs](directions/motion-design/payoffs.md) exists.

Give no single numeric score: a beautiful score cannot offset an unreadable demonstration, and a clever ending cannot repair a dishonest promise.

## Three passes on every cut

- **First read**: a new viewer identifies the important action without being told where to look.
- **Mode**: watch a reel muted to expose clutter or missing information (an explainer may still need its words); judge a music video or visual score with its audio, since it need not work silent.
- **Structure**: for a story or explanation, compare the opening and closing relationship or understanding; for music, compare each recurring section with its sound; for a loop, watch several cycles across the return.

## Form finish tests

Ask a viewer who has not seen the piece, without first explaining what it means.

| Form | Question | Weak answer means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative | What happened, and why? | Causes or stakes are not on screen |
| Explainer | Explain or solve this new case. | They hold an example, not the mechanism |
| Music video | How do the images relate to the track, and where does that weaken? | No specific musical property drives the picture |
| Documentary or essay | Which parts are testimony and which are interpretation? | Reconstruction reads as evidence |
| Poem or visual essay | Which images or relationships stayed with you? | The images share a style but no relation |
| Loop or ambient | Does the repeated experience stay coherent and pleasurable? | The return jars or the state does not hold |
| Promo | What is the offer, and what would you do next? | The product has no necessary role in the payoff |

## Troubleshooting

One symptom can have several causes; treat each row as a hypothesis to test, not a verdict.

| Symptom | Likely cause | First fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The opening feels slow | Nothing relevant starts before the explanation | Enter on the first meaningful action; cut the preamble |
| Clear but uninteresting | No outcome is uncertain or worth wanting | Strengthen the form's driver (a goal, a useful question, a musical relation, an image association, a sensory pattern); do not add conflict by default |
| Impressive but empty | Only the imagery changes | Give each scene a consequence or each movement a developing formal relation |
| The middle repeats | Attempts do not change the next available option | Change strategy, cost or relationship; delete an interchangeable attempt |
| The ending feels rushed | Redundant setups crowd it | Remove setups before shortening the payoff |
| The twist feels unfair | The true facts contradict an earlier scene | Plant a normally visible clue; remove contradicting evidence |
| The emotion feels forced | The relationship did not matter before the event | Add one specific earlier interaction; reduce explanatory music or dialogue |
| The climax is hard to follow | Rule, geography or alternatives are unclear | Establish them before the fast action; show the decisive gesture |
| The joke is remembered, the product is not | The offer has no necessary role in the payoff | Tie its real action to the turn; make attribution readable earlier |
| The lyrics sound awkward | Natural stress and breath fight the melody | Speak the line, simplify it, rebuild the phrase on the grid in [music](directions/motion-design/music.md) |
| Enjoyable lesson, cannot be applied | The viewer has a memorable example, not the mechanism | Add a fresh case with less guidance and explain its answer |
| The metaphor creates a misconception | An imagined property transferred wrongly | Show the real correspondence and where it stops |
| The tutorial can be followed, not repeated | Decisions and starting conditions were hidden | Show intermediate states; include an independent attempt |
| The song is remembered, the lesson is not | The refrain replaced the explanation | Keep the hook, then demonstrate and apply the relation |
| The music video feels generic | No specific musical property controls the visuals | Build around a recurring phrase, gesture or relationship ([C10](directions/motion-design/composition-forms.md)) |
| The visualiser feels too busy | It is being held to a story's escalation rule | Reduce salient changes; strengthen atmosphere and continuity |
| The poem feels arbitrary | The images share a style, not a relation | Develop a coherent image family without forcing one reading |
| Liked but rarely shared | No specific recipient or reason to send it | Sharpen recognition, usefulness or a retellable payoff; more share requests do not fix it |
| Little reach despite good feedback | The intended audience never met an honest, appealing promise | Inspect distribution, packaging and audience fit before discarding the story |

## Audience feedback

Method: rehearse cheaply before the full build: play the boards or animatic in order with dialogue read and lyrics sung at the planned pace → show it to a few people new to it, ideally from the intended audience → ask the form question above, plus what the piece is about, what they expect next and which detail made them care → check they can read the first image on the intended screen → turn answers into a short list of specific fixes.

Move on when essential comprehension works and the remaining disagreements are creative choices. A handful of viewers gives qualitative fixes, not proof of wide appeal. Record each round on a render through the `review` action.

## After release

Separate the questions, since one metric cannot answer all of them:

- **Reach**: did people have the chance to see it?
- **Entry**: did the title, cover and opening make the intended audience start?
- **Continuation**: where did attention hold, drop or return? Look for drops at context dumps, unreadable frames and delayed answers. A retention spike can mean enjoyment or confusion that forced a rewatch; inspect the scene before calling it success.
- **Outcome for the form**: a lesson's viewers can apply the idea to an unfamiliar case; an expressive piece supports listening, interpretation or repeat viewing; a promo's viewers understood the offer and took the next action.

Compare against the channel's own pieces of similar format, length, objective and audience, noting differences in distribution, paid promotion and audience size; never against a viral outlier. Next time change the single largest plausible weakness (opening, middle repetition, payoff, product connection or distribution); one upload is too small a sample to rebuild a style around. Keep the mechanism that worked (a recurring behaviour, reward or structure) and vary the subject, obstacle, setting and ending.
