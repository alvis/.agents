# History and culture recipes: true stories

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Make the past surprising without making it false: the comic premise earns attention, the causal explanation earns trust.

## Accuracy rules

- Verify every fact of your subject in authoritative sources; a reference film models format, never facts.
- Tag each script claim as sourced, reconstructed, comic, or speculative, and give each tag its own visual or narration treatment.
- Invented dialogue reads as comic dramatisation, never as an archival quote. Never invent private thoughts, diagnoses, quotations, or casualty numbers.
- Aim jokes at incentives, misunderstandings, and scale; never make suffering the punchline.
- Name disagreements between historians when they change the answer.
- Timings are proportional starting allocations for a 60 s cut, refit to the bar grid. A longer cut adds chapters that each deliver an answer, never extra jokes on a one-minute fact.

## HIS1 · Absurd true incident

- Use when: a documented response dwarfed its trigger, as when a shot pig on a disputed island led to a British-American military standoff over an existing boundary dispute.
- Ingredients: trivial trigger, underlying tension, named decision-makers, documented outcome.
- Method: trigger in the foreground, disproportionate response behind it → rewind only to the real pressure → a sensible-looking first response → its unintended consequence → a costlier response → documented resolution → callback to the trigger. Alternate a stable map with short character exchanges; each joke exposes an incentive or misunderstanding; a solemn cue cut short by a deadpan reaction carries the absurdity.
- Timing: mismatch 0–4 s, trigger 4–13 s, real cause 13–25 s, escalation 25–40 s, resolution 40–52 s, callback 52–60 s.
- Variations: a longer cut gives competing claims, local incentives, escalation, and negotiation a chapter each.
- Fix: history merely looks silly; show why each response seemed rational to the people making it.
- Finish test: Can the viewer retell both the absurd incident and the serious reason it escalated?

## HIS2 · One historical why

- Use when: a narrow question has a surprising answer, such as why powers with reasons to join a war stayed out.
- Ingredients: one apparent contradiction, a few plausible explanations, the decisive context, a small cast.
- Method: expected action crossed out beside what happened, puzzle before any timeline → the tempting explanation → each actor's incentives and constraints, one change at a time → the combination that explains the choices → the title answered in one sentence. Labelled cutouts on a fixed map; deadpan reactions give comedy without breaking the causal chain.
- Timing: puzzle 0–6 s, tempting explanation 6–15 s, incentives 15–48 s, combination 48–55 s, answer 55–60 s.
- Variations: add [DOC2](directions/motion-design/recipes/mystery-real.md)'s map-and-evidence discipline for a geopolitical essay.
- Fix: the episode drifts into a full history of the war; cut every event that does not bear on the question.
- Finish test: Can the viewer answer the title question?

## HIS3 · Rapid musical overview

- Use when: a broad sweep needs an intelligible trajectory with memorable landmarks.
- Ingredients: starting state, turning points linked by cause, one narrator, a few recurring musical or visual signals.
- Method: starting state plus a glimpse of the journey's scale → a pressure → the map or system transforms → punctuate the change → jump to the next causal threshold → a recurring image returns at larger scale. Map, words, and music perform as one; a short sung motif marks one recurring idea, not every noun.
- Timing: equal bars per threshold, punctuation on downbeats, and a one-bar rest after each dense passage so the next lands fresh.
- Variations: sweep a technology, language, or city instead of the world.
- Fix: speed without causal links becomes a fact montage; state the cause behind every jump. Borrow musical punctuation, not a creator's jingles, wording, or look.
- Finish test: Can the viewer narrate the trajectory landmark by landmark?

## HIS4 · Biography through a defining contradiction

- Use when: a person's ambition, public image, and consequences do not align.
- Ingredients: the contradiction, starting constraints, a few consequential choices, the environment that rewarded them, legacy.
- Method: early ambition beside a sharply different later consequence → limited starting circumstances → a consequential choice → the environment that rewards it → accumulating power or cost → the next decisive choice → legacy and its limits. One recurring object or gesture changes meaning; music marks shifts in perspective, not moral verdicts.
- Timing: contradiction 0–6 s, circumstances 6–12 s, two choice cycles 12–48 s, legacy 48–60 s.
- Variations: a founder or institution can replace the person; mature subjects keep humour away from atrocities.
- Fix: childhood made the outcome inevitable, or one person caused it alone; tie every choice visibly to institutions and circumstances.
- Finish test: Does the viewer see how person and context interacted?

## Shelf finish test

Would the viewer know which parts are sourced, reconstructed, comic, or speculative?
