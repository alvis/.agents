# History and culture recipes: everyday worlds

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Day-in-the-life, object, myth, and alternate-history forms (HIS5–HIS8). Apply the accuracy rules and shelf finish test in [history: true stories](directions/motion-design/recipes/history-true.md).

## HIS5 · Day in the life

- Use when: an ordinary historical role can be shown through one concrete task under a grounded constraint.
- Ingredients: a role, a task, the rules of the time, a composite character labelled as a reconstruction where used.
- Method: the task and its rules, not a panorama → wake or arrive → a practical problem → institutions met by doing → a consequential choice → its result → step outside the character to say what was typical and what was exceptional. Follow a route through a few recurring spaces; reveal food, tools, and clothing only when the task needs them; narration marks evidence boundaries.
- Timing: task 0–5 s, the day 5–50 s, typical-versus-exceptional coda 50–60 s.
- Variations: the Day in the life device in [story devices](references/story-devices.md), with historical constraints in place of traits.
- Fix: one person reads as everyone; show differences in status and access. Modern jokes stay in narration, never as documented ancient speech.
- Finish test: Can the viewer imagine this life and say what about it was not typical?

## HIS6 · Secret life of an everyday object

- Use when: a familiar drink, tool, garment, or habit has a chain of changing uses, routes, and meanings.
- Ingredients: the object today, its origin or the uncertainty about it, one transformation, a movement between communities, a conflict or adaptation.
- Method: object in a modern scene, then its unexpected earlier use → origin or uncertainty → one transformation → movement between places → conflict or adaptation → back to the object in the viewer's hands. Match-cut its silhouette across settings; map only movements that change the explanation; let sound follow materials and ritual, not geographic stereotypes.
- Timing: hook 0–5 s, origin 5–15 s, transformation and movement 15–45 s, adaptation 45–53 s, return 53–60 s.
- Variations: a gesture or custom works like a physical object.
- Fix: a single inventor forced onto a distributed development, legend shown as archaeological evidence, or exchange shown as one-way; separate legend from evidence on screen.
- Finish test: Does the viewer now see the object differently?

## HIS7 · Myth with a modern echo

- Use when: a specific traditional tale has a rule, a transgression, and an image that still resonates.
- Ingredients: named tradition and variant, the tale's rules, a desire, the transgression, the enduring image.
- Method: the impossible predicament first → the rule → the character's desire → an attempted loophole → the consequence → the opening image again → one labelled interpretation or modern parallel. Show the rule as a repeatable visual pattern, break it when the character cheats, restore it with changed meaning at the end.
- Timing: predicament 0–5 s, tale 5–48 s, return 48–53 s, interpretation 53–60 s.
- Variations: fables and founding legends take the same build.
- Fix: tale, later philosophical readings, and your own reading blur; label each layer, and never present myth as a record.
- Finish test: Does the viewer know the tale and why it stays with people?

## HIS8 · Alternate history

- Use when: one changed historical condition can reveal what real history depended on.
- Ingredients: credible real baseline, one explicit divergence, several conditional branches.
- Method: a familiar world with one major absence, marked as alternate at once → brief real baseline → the divergence → nearest consequences → second-order effects → at least one plausible alternative branch → what the comparison reveals about real history. Recorded history is a solid line, speculation a distinct treatment whose uncertainty grows with distance from the divergence.
- Timing: hook 0–5 s, baseline 5–12 s, near consequences 12–30 s, second-order 30–42 s, alternative branch 42–52 s, lesson 52–60 s.
- Variations: a changed physical rule belongs to [DIS1](directions/motion-design/recipes/science-mechanisms.md).
- Fix: invented outcomes slip into factual narration, or other actors behave as in the real timeline; keep speculation in its own register and let every actor respond.
- Finish test: Does the viewer understand a dependency rather than believe a prediction?
