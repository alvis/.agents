# Visual essay, documentary and loop recipes

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Six forms ordered by an organising principle instead of a character pursuing a goal. Every timing is a starting allocation for the runtime it names.

## Choose the organising principle

| Purpose | Recipe | What sets the order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Let someone's real account be heard | X1 | The speaker's meaning and evidentiary limits |
| Examine an idea or interpretation | X2 | The reasoning |
| Interpret a poem or spoken text | X3 | Language, imagery and associations |
| Satisfy through a visible process | X4 | The process and its rhythm |
| Present a collection or compare alternatives | X5 | A stable comparison dimension |
| Introduce a show, event, brand or world | X6 | The promise of what follows |

Data-led explanation belongs to [E7](directions/motion-design/recipes/explainer-practice.md). Abstract visual music belongs to [V2 or V7](directions/motion-design/recipes/music-video.md).

## X1 · Voice, Image, Context

- Use when: animated testimony, personal documentary, an interview extract, or an account with no usable or appropriate footage.
- Ingredients: an authorised source account; its context; a marked distinction between remembered, observed and imagined material; one visual interpretation; a limited scope.
- Method: find the account's central thought and keep the source wording beside every edit so the speaker's meaning survives → tag each statement as direct testimony, independently established fact or interpretation, and decide what each image claims to show → animate memory, feeling, relationship or the unavailable view, labelling reconstruction wherever it could pass for a recording → place needed context beside the account without forcing pauses into twists → stop where the thought reaches a meaningful end; a reflective question is fine, invented resolution for a real person is not.
- Timing: for 4:00, 0:00–0:30 voice and context; 0:30–2:45 account and imagery; 2:45–3:30 context or qualification; 3:30–4:00 closing reflection. Reorder blocks when the source needs it.
- Variations: first-person diary, several attributed voices, an object accompanying testimony, an annotated interview. Keep real source audio distinct from reconstruction.
- Fix: if emotional animation seems to prove what it only imagines, label the reconstruction and separate interpretation from corroboration.
- Finish test: Can a viewer tell whose account this is and which parts are visual interpretation?

## X2 · Question, Evidence, Reading, Qualification

- Use when: a cultural explanation, design argument, research-informed essay or exploration of an idea.
- Ingredients: one question; a bounded proposition; selected examples; a line of reasoning; a real limit or alternative reading.
- Method: pose the question without promising a stronger answer than the material supports → introduce a concrete example the viewer can inspect → show the inferential step from example to reading, not music implying the conclusion is obvious → add a contrasting example, limit or counterexample that genuinely tests the idea → close on the refined understanding: what seems supported, under which conditions, and what stays open.
- Timing: for 5:00, 0:00–0:30 question; 0:30–1:35 example; 1:35–2:45 reasoning; 2:45–4:05 contrast; 4:05–5:00 refined conclusion.
- Variations: an interpretive essay can invite several readings; a factual one still separates documented claims from interpretation. Keep evidence visually distinct from invented symbols.
- Fix: if confident assertions merely resemble an argument, insert the missing evidence-to-conclusion steps and narrow unsupported claims.
- Finish test: Can someone tell what the film shows, what it infers and what it only asks?

## X3 · Image Family and Associative Return

- Use when: a visual poem, spoken-word film, reflective animation or language-led experimental piece.
- Ingredients: an original or authorised text; one family of related images; a vocal rhythm; chosen word-picture relationships; a recurring motif.
- Method: read the text for turns of thought, sensory relations and silences, never as a noun-by-noun illustration list → choose one visual family that can develop the text's concerns → decide where the picture accompanies, extends or contradicts the words → return to chosen motifs in changed contexts, or hold them long enough to invite a new reading → end on an image or interval that leaves room for resonance.
- Timing: the spoken performance sets the duration; mark the opening image, turns of thought and final hold, and never stretch or hurry the reader to fit a plot template.
- Variations: a fixed refrain over changing imagery, repeated imagery under changing words, or silent gaps that become part of the poem. Let speech cadence shape visual phrases.
- Fix: if each line gets an unrelated surreal image, establish recurring relations or simplify around the strongest verbal turns.
- Finish test: Can you say why the images belong together without insisting on one literal message?

## X4 · Rule, Progress, Completion, Return

- Use when: a satisfying process animation, assembly, sorting, transformation or short rhythmic loop.
- Ingredients: one legible rule; visible progress; an appealing completion; a natural return or deliberate stop.
- Method: show enough of the rule that viewers can picture the completed state → develop through observable change along one main dimension → add a variation only when it makes progress more rewarding; conflict is not required → show the completion plainly rather than hiding it behind the loop point → stop on the result or start a new cycle through a consistent reset.
- Timing: for 15 s, 0–2 s rule; 2–10 s progress; 10–13 s completion; 13–15 s return or hold.
- Variations: no twist, one elegant exception, nested cycles, or a silent loop. A sound can mark completion or continuity; a beat does not need everything moving at once. Imaginary physics must not pose as a real demonstration of a factual process.
- Fix: if an endlessly deferred result feels like bait, give a complete local reward before the next cycle. A reset is a defect only when it cancels a promised narrative consequence, not because the loop is cyclical.
- Finish test: Is the cycle enjoyable and understandable on first viewing, with an intentional reset?

## X5 · One Lens, Many Variations

- Use when: a collection reveal, taxonomy, comparative explainer, product family or curated showcase.
- Ingredients: one comparison question; a small set of examples; consistent presentation; a meaningful order; a synthesis.
- Method: define what varies and what stays comparable → set the presentation with the first example so viewers know where to look → bring every later example through the same viewpoint, units and anchors, changing context only to reveal a relevant difference → use a contrasting or boundary example to sharpen the organising idea → finish on the whole collection or choice, now understandable.
- Timing: for 30 s, 0–3 s lens; 3–9 s first example; 9–15 s second; 15–21 s third; 21–26 s synthesis; 26–30 s finish or invitation.
- Variations: increasing scale, a spectrum, opposing approaches or a family resemblance; add examples only for new distinctions. Vary one musical phrase rather than resetting the identity per item.
- Fix: if the montage becomes a catalogue of unrelated features, choose one question every example genuinely answers.
- Finish test: Can viewers state the organising idea rather than list the objects?

## X6 · Identity Overture

- Use when: a title sequence, programme opening, event ident or launch piece that introduces an experience rather than explains a product.
- Ingredients: a clear tone or proposition; two or three recurring motifs; a rhythm of arrival; a readable identity; an honest relation to the work that follows.
- Method: define the promised experience: curiosity, precision, play, tension, intimacy or scale → introduce a motif that embodies it rather than decorating the name → develop it through a few related variations, leaving room and reading time for required titles → bring the motifs into a recognisable identity or final composition → hand over cleanly without spending a climax the main work has not earned.
- Timing: for 20 s, 0–3 s premise; 3–12 s motif development; 12–17 s identity arrival; 17–20 s hold or handover.
- Variations: minimal ident, elaborate title sequence, recurring episode opener or modular event opening; a sonic identity can tie the overture to later chapters.
- Fix: if the opening promises a different kind of show, rebuild its motifs and rhythm around the actual content.
- Finish test: Does the opening set a truthful expectation and a memorable identity without a miniature plot?
