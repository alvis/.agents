# Explainer recipes: correction, practice and evidence

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

E4 to E8. Choose the recipe and apply the accuracy rules and length allocations in [mechanisms](directions/motion-design/recipes/explainer-mechanism.md) first. Every timing is a starting allocation for the runtime it names.

## E4 · Tempting Explanation, Decisive Test, Better Model

- Use when: a misconception, oversimplified model or common reasoning error.
- Ingredients: an accurately stated misconception; why it seems plausible; one discriminating example; a better explanation; a reusable check.
- Method: pose the real question, stating the correct direction first when the misconception alone could mislead → show why the intuitive answer tempts, without mocking → present a case where the two explanations predict different outcomes, under equivalent conditions → say what the result supports and what stays uncertain, replacing the old model rather than only calling it wrong → give a new case and a short question the viewer can use to check their reasoning.
- Timing: for 3:00, 0:00–0:20 question; 0:20–0:50 intuitive model; 0:50–1:35 decisive example; 1:35–2:30 replacement; 2:30–3:00 check.
- Variations: "usually, except when" can beat true or false; the better model may narrow a claim rather than overturn it.
- Fix: if the false statement becomes the slogan, make the corrected principle the recurring line and closing image, with at least as clear a visual identity.
- Finish test: Can the viewer explain why the old answer fails, not just name the right one?

## E5 · Show One, Coach One, Try One

- Use when: a tutorial, practical skill, reasoning procedure or worked mathematical example.
- Ingredients: one target task; prerequisites; a complete worked example; a similar coached case; an independent attempt; feedback.
- Method: show the goal, the starting state and what counts as success → work one example, giving the decision behind each step and showing each resulting state → present a similar case with fewer prompts and invite the next step before revealing it → change surface details for an independent attempt, with the answer after a real pause and no new facts or countdown in that space → explain the answer and one common error, then end on the compact method and its limits.
- Timing: for 6:00, 0:00–0:30 goal and prerequisites; 0:30–2:40 worked example; 2:40–4:05 coached attempt; 4:05–5:10 independent attempt; 5:10–6:00 feedback and recap.
- Variations: a ten-second micro-demonstration can teach one action but cannot promise the competence of a guided lesson.
- Fix: if viewers can follow but not repeat, remove guidance in the next example and watch where independent action fails.
- Finish test: Can the learner complete a new case without the narrator doing the reasoning?

## E6 · Anchor, Change Scale, Reconnect

- Use when: size, time, quantity, nested systems, or local versus global behaviour.
- Ingredients: a familiar reference; clearly labelled scales or levels; consistent transitions; one question that stays relevant throughout.
- Method: establish the reference and the quantity compared → change one scale or level at a time, announcing switches of space, time or representation, with a persistent scale marker → at each stop reveal what can be understood only there → stop at the level that answers the question → return to the starting reference with the relationship visible.
- Timing: for 5:00, 0:00–0:35 anchor; 0:35–1:00 scale rule; 1:00–3:40 selected levels; 3:40–4:25 connection; 4:25–5:00 return.
- Variations: physical scale, timescale or level of organisation; these are different axes, so label a switch rather than disguising it as one zoom. Expanding music may express wonder but must not imply bigger means more important.
- Fix: if the zoom teaches no relation, write each stop's explanatory job and cut empty stops.
- Finish test: Can the viewer describe the relationship beyond "very big" or "very small"?

## E7 · Human Question, Data, Interpretation, Limit

- Use when: an animated chart, research summary or evidence-led visual essay.
- Ingredients: one question; an appropriate source; defined units and population; a baseline; a visible pattern; uncertainty and scope.
- Method: state the question and why this measure helps answer it → introduce axes, units, population and timeframe before data moves → reveal the comparison or change against a stable reference, keeping scale changes explicit and marking estimates or illustrative values → separate observation, proposed mechanism and causal evidence → show a real limitation or alternative explanation, then end on the supported conclusion.
- Timing: for 4:00, 0:00–0:30 question; 0:30–1:00 reading the representation; 1:00–2:15 pattern; 2:15–3:10 interpretation; 3:10–4:00 limits and conclusion.
- Variations: compare groups, trace change over time or explain a distribution; choose the chart for the question, not for its animation.
- Fix: if a dramatic statistic lacks context, restore the denominator, baseline, date and source beside the claim.
- Finish test: Can the viewer say what was measured and name a conclusion the evidence does not justify?

## E8 · A Situation That Requires the Lesson

- Use when: teaching through a story, an animated case study or a character-led puzzle.
- Ingredients: one learning objective; a situation where it matters; a decision; consequences governed by the real principle; a return to the general rule.
- Method: write the accurate explanation, then build a situation around a decision it genuinely governs → give a person a small concrete task with no competing villain or backstory → let an intuitive strategy fail because of the real principle → explain the principle with a diagram, comparison or worked action at the moment it becomes useful, keeping story props distinct from the literal model → the character applies it, then a changed context invites the viewer to apply it.
- Timing: for 6:00, 0:00–0:40 situation; 0:40–1:35 first strategy; 1:35–3:35 explanation; 3:35–4:45 application; 4:45–6:00 transfer and general rule.
- Variations: a guide voices the viewer's questions, the viewer decides, or an object is personified without pretending the process has intentions. Example: comparing three quarters with two thirds moves from slices to twelve equal units, nine twelfths against eight.
- Fix: if a mascot lectures while an unrelated story plays, make the decision depend on the lesson or remove the character.
- Finish test: Would the ending change if the character learned the wrong principle? If not, the story is not teaching.
