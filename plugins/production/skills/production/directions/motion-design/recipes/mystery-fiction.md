# Mystery and documentary recipes: invented worlds

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Speculative documentary, analog horror, found footage, and series-pilot forms (DOC5–DOC8). Apply the evidence and fiction contract and shelf finish test in [mystery: real cases](directions/motion-design/recipes/mystery-real.md).

## DOC5 · Speculative nature documentary or world tour

- Use when: an invented environment has consistent constraints from which organisms or artifacts follow.
- Ingredients: an environmental rule, one organism or artifact, its adaptation, an interaction, a larger pattern, an open frontier.
- Method: one startling adaptation, fictional premise named early → the environmental rule → the organism and its adaptation → interaction with another part of the system → the larger ecological or cultural pattern → a frontier worth exploring. Alternate habitat views with details; maps, field notes, specimen labels, and ambient sound give documentary tone with few speakers.
- Timing: adaptation 0–6 s, rule and organism 6–24 s, interaction 24–40 s, pattern 40–52 s, frontier 52–60 s.
- Variations: a museum of fictional artifacts needs almost no characters.
- Fix: speculative biology reads as science, or protected fictional designs are borrowed; design your own world and keep the label visible.
- Finish test: Does the world feel coherent enough to explore beyond this episode?

## DOC6 · Analog horror or corrupted public information

- Use when: a believable fictional institution's mundane communication can turn wrong.
- Ingredients: the institution's voice, a mundane broadcast or training-screen format, an instruction growing wrong.
- Method: a calm, recognisable layout → the institution's voice → a small anomaly → the message repeated with a consequential change → a disturbing rule → an earlier detail reinterpreted → a complete local discovery. Degradation stays purposeful and text readable; a slight timing error or missing sound unsettles more than constant glitches.
- Timing: calm layout 0–12 s, anomaly 12–20 s, repetitions 20–44 s, rule and reinterpretation 44–56 s, discovery 56–60 s.
- Variations: a corporate training video or public-service announcement can be the format.
- Fix: a random jump scare ends an unrelated build-up; make the payoff reinterpret earlier material.
- Finish test: Does earlier material gain a second meaning?

## DOC7 · Found footage or liminal exploration

- Use when: a familiar space can stop behaving normally around a camera-holder with a goal.
- Ingredients: an immediate goal, a comprehensible environment, a strange rule, a limited viewpoint.
- Method: ordinary activity → an impossible transition the viewer notices before the camera reacts → exploration that teaches the geography → evidence of another presence or rule → a choice governed by that rule → escape, capture, or discovery. Every camera move has a reason; keep landmarks so disorientation is deliberate; off-screen sound directs attention without announcing the threat.
- Timing: activity 0–8 s, transition 8–14 s, exploration 14–36 s, evidence 36–46 s, choice and outcome 46–60 s.
- Variations: [Blender](directions/motion-design/blender.md) suits a handheld camera through a 3D space.
- Fix: endless corridors replace development; give each section a new rule, landmark, or choice.
- Finish test: Can the viewer say what changed and why the final action mattered?

## DOC8 · Original series pilot

- Use when: a newcomer or destabilising event can open a strange world worth more episodes.
- Ingredients: the world's routine, someone who rejects its rules, a few distinct relationships, one local objective, a larger world question.
- Method: the world's routine, interrupted → an immediate practical goal → test one rule → a relationship found through action → a local crisis resolved → the broader limitation that earns another episode. Give each major location and relationship a clear visual identity; an opening theme sets a confidence the interruption breaks; cast only who this conflict needs.
- Timing: routine 0–8 s, interruption 8–14 s, goal and rule 14–34 s, relationship and crisis 34–52 s, world question 52–60 s.
- Variations: a short can be a cold open ending on the world question.
- Fix: lore terminology replaces an opening situation, or every answer is deferred; resolve the local crisis now.
- Finish test: Did the viewer finish a story and want more of the world?
