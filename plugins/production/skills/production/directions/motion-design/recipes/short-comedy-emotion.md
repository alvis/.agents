# Short animation recipes: comedy and emotional change

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

The short shelf holds ten writing recipes for character-led and dramatic shorts of 3 to 10 minutes. Fill the slots with the user's subject; each recipe should yield a different film when its ingredients change. Every timing is a starting allocation for the runtime it names.

## Shelf rules

- Pick by the experience the viewer should have, not by how impressive the premise sounds. Comedy and reversal: A1, A7. Emotional change: A2, A3, A4 (this file). Curiosity, suspense, wonder: A5, A6, A8. No conventional plot: A9, A10 ([curiosity and open forms](directions/motion-design/recipes/short-curiosity.md)).
- At 3 minutes, keep one main relationship or rule, fewer attempts and little backstory; protect the payoff before any extra setup scene.
- At 6 minutes, spend the extra time on a changed understanding, a meaningful second attempt or a relationship shift, not on longer travel between scenes.
- At 10 minutes, add a second development only when it changes the first. More time supports an ensemble or a weightier choice; it does not require a subplot.
- To combine, use one recipe as the spine and another as a local device: A3 can contain an A1 run of failed help; A6 can end on an A4 callback. Never give one short three independent climaxes.
- To make it distinctive, keep the structure and replace the obvious desire, behaviour, relationship detail and outcome.

## A1 · Three Attempts and a Reversal

- Use when: a readable, mostly wordless comedy with one goal, a small cast and one location; a good first short. Runs 3 to 5 minutes.
- Ingredients: one concrete goal; one stable obstacle; two different failed strategies; an expectation for the third attempt; one planted detail that makes the ending fair.
- Method: show goal and obstacle in one scene, no life history → the obvious attempt fails and teaches how this world works → a cleverer attempt fails differently, raising embarrassment, cost or collateral rather than size → the third attempt starts recognisably, then the planted detail turns it into unexpected success, self-defeat or success with an ironic catch → hold the reaction and end before anyone explains the joke.
- Timing: for 3:00, 0:00–0:20 goal; 0:20–0:55 first attempt; 0:55–1:40 second; 1:40–2:35 third and turn; 2:35–3:00 consequence.
- Variations: a collaborative final strategy for warmth, a deliberately foolish one for farce, a near success for a bittersweet end.
- Fix: if the three gags feel interchangeable, make each attempt change the options left for the next.
- Finish test: Can the viewer predict the kind of attempt without predicting its exact result?

## A7 · One-Upmanship and Consequence

- Use when: a competitive duo, status comedy, rivalry or chain of retaliatory gags. Runs 3 to 6 minutes.
- Ingredients: two incompatible wants; one shared resource or arena; a recognisable move and countermove; a consequence both parties overlook at first.
- Method: make both wants visible, giving the opponent a motive even if petty → the first character gains a small advantage and the response exploits that exact move → alternate control so each move hands material to the next → escalate until winning the local contest threatens dignity, the shared resource or the relationship → end in earned cooperation, deserved reversal or mutual comic defeat, chosen before writing the middle.
- Timing: for 5:00, 0:00–0:35 wants; 0:35–1:25 first exchange; 1:25–3:20 escalation; 3:20–4:25 consequence; 4:25–5:00 changed relationship or final gag.
- Variations: unequal skill makes an underdog comedy, equal skill a duel, a group against one a social hierarchy.
- Fix: if a random punishment ends the quarrel, make the rivals build the mechanism of their own consequence.
- Finish test: Could the final accident happen without the rivalry? If yes, reconnect it causally.

## A2 · Fear to Discovery

- Use when: an uplifting coming-of-age story, a first attempt or an intimidating unfamiliar world. Runs 4 to 7 minutes.
- Ingredients: a modest need; a specific fear; a first failure; a new observation; an achievable changed action.
- Method: show why the character must engage with the frightening situation → make the first failure understandable so the fear comes from experience, not a timid face → let avoidance cost something or leave the need unmet → give access to new information by observing, experimenting or accepting help → return to the same obstacle, show the changed behaviour, then let the character use it alone.
- Timing: for 6:00, 0:00–0:40 need; 0:40–1:40 encounter; 1:40–2:40 avoidance; 2:40–4:05 discovery; 4:05–5:25 new attempt; 5:25–6:00 result.
- Variations: the breakthrough can be perceptual, social or practical; the obstacle may stay genuinely hard.
- Fix: if a pep talk cures the fear, replace the speech with one observable discovery and a risky but manageable attempt.
- Finish test: What can the character now notice or do that they could not at the start?

## A3 · Small Task, Big Relationship

- Use when: warmth, family, friendship, care, or a brand film about helping. Runs 4 to 8 minutes.
- Ingredients: a task that matters to someone; a helper who misunderstands at first; a recipient with preferences; an adjustment; a modest result.
- Method: show why this task matters to the recipient → the helper tries their default method and fails → the failure reveals something about the relationship → the recipient contributes, refuses or redirects, and the helper learns to attend rather than impose → finish the task differently and end on an action expressing the changed relationship, not praise.
- Timing: for 6:00, 0:00–0:40 task; 0:40–2:10 failed help; 2:10–3:30 friction; 3:30–5:10 adjustment; 5:10–6:00 result.
- Variations: reverse who teaches whom; leave the practical result imperfect; make asking for help the hard action.
- Fix: if the sentiment is generic, replace "they care" with a gesture, preference or detail only these people would share.
- Finish test: Does the recipient keep agency, or only make the helper look good?

## A4 · Repeated Ritual, Changed Meaning

- Use when: memory, growing apart, affection, grief, or a relationship across time. Runs 3 to 7 minutes.
- Ingredients: one repeatable action; a meaningful object or place; two established versions of the ritual; a changed circumstance; a final variation.
- Method: choose an ordinary action that expresses the relationship without explanation → repeat it with a variation that reveals personality, making it worth noticing before any loss → alter the circumstance through age, distance, responsibility, absence or new understanding → repeat the recognisable action, changing who performs it, who receives it or what is left empty → stop on the audience's recognition without summarising the theme.
- Timing: for 5:00, 0:00–0:45 first ritual; 0:45–1:50 second variation; 1:50–3:15 changed circumstance; 3:15–4:20 final ritual; 4:20–5:00 consequence and hold.
- Variations: the ritual passes to another generation, becomes reciprocal or is deliberately abandoned. Death is not required.
- Fix: if a sad event supplies all the emotion, make the earlier ritual enjoyable and particular enough to matter on its own.
- Finish test: Would a viewer recognise the change with the final explanatory sentence removed?
