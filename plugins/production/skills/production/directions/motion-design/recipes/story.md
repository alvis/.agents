# Narrative and emotion

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

## Emotional honesty

- Begin with something someone wants; the way they pursue it reveals what the film is about.
- Setup: an ordinary action makes the desire visible. Development: an attempt changes the relationship or understanding. Payoff: a repeated action now means something different.
- Replace an explanatory line with an observable choice wherever possible. Test action for geography, a reveal for fairness, and emotion for whether the relationship matters before the music swells.
- Cut an animatic with temporary sound before polishing character animation.
- Match distressing themes to the intended audience.
- Timings are starting allocations; most assume a 2–5 min short.

## STO1 · The small task that means love
- Use when: care is best shown through a practical task someone struggles to do.
- Ingredients: a modest goal; a strained relationship; a visible obstacle; a reason the task matters beyond its mechanics.
- Method: someone determined at a task they cannot yet manage, with one quiet clue it matters → inadequate first attempt → frustration → vulnerability or help → changed method → finished task as an act of care → context that deepens it. Stage: hands, tools, and reactions readable; return to the same action after the emotional change; music follows the relationship.
- Timing: task and attempts 50%, turn 20%, completion and context 30%.
- Variations: low-character version with only hands, an object, and a voice or recording.
- Fix: a final revelation carrying all the emotion; make each attempt matter first.
- Finish test: does the completed task read as love without the reveal?

## STO2 · Empathy reversal / reframe the first impression
- Use when: a first judgement should be revised by fair context.
- Ingredients: an initial rejection; behaviour that challenges it; a contextual reveal; an action that repeats.
- Method: someone rejects what should delight them, unexplained → the rejected party acts on its own → persistence changes the relationship → context revealed through action → an earlier action repeats with a different response → changed behaviour. Stage: plant clues in ordinary staging, never misleading cuts; a recurring sound or gesture gains warmth on return.
- Timing: judgement 20%, persistence 45%, reveal and repeat 35%.
- Variations: the viewer, not a character, holds the first judgement.
- Fix: difference or vulnerability used as a shock twist or for pity; give the characters agency before and after the reveal.
- Finish test: rewatched, were the clues fair?

## STO3 · Emotion made physical / romantic pursuit
- Use when: a hidden feeling can take a visible form with its own rule.
- Ingredients: a feeling to hide; its physical embodiment with a clear rule; a real social risk.
- Method: composure and the feeling's misbehaviour in one frame → desire → concealment → the feeling acts → escalating control attempts → exposure → the other person's response → a new relationship to the feeling. Stage: one consistent motion language for the embodiment; separate what the protagonist sees from what the viewer notices; a musical motif can carry the feeling instead of speech.
- Timing: setup 20%, escalation 50%, exposure and response 30%.
- Variations: any feeling (fear, pride, grief) with the same embodiment rule.
- Fix: persistence framed as entitlement; respect the other character's agency, and keep the metaphor readable as it develops.
- Finish test: does the viewer feel vulnerability rather than hear a lecture on it?

## STO4 · Impossible dream / meaning revealed by labour
- Use when: puzzling, purposeful work hides a desire the finished construction reveals.
- Ingredients: an unusual preparation; a coherent sequence of effort; a desire the result makes legible; its emotional price.
- Method: work whose purpose is withheld but whose every action reads → repeated effort with variation → rising cost → the structure becomes legible → the desired moment → a final image of the price. Stage: close shots for labour; save the wide reveal for when scale changes meaning; the final sensory moment answers the preparation.
- Timing: preparation and effort 60%, reveal and moment 30%, final image 10%.
- Variations: a gift or surprise built in secret for someone else.
- Fix: repetition that pads instead of showing commitment; vary each pass. Never present self-endangerment as a shortcut to fulfilment.
- Finish test: is the purpose intelligible the moment it is revealed?

## STO5 · Philosophical dialogue / speculative fable
- Use when: an abstract question can be made personal through one impossible rule.
- Ingredients: a concrete predicament; two contrasting perspectives; one impossible rule; questions testing it.
- Method: a situation that makes the question personal → first explanation → practical objection → wider implication → challenge to values → return to the personal concern, transformed. Stage: start in a small intimate space and widen scale only as the argument widens; let questions breathe.
- Timing: predicament 15%, argument 65%, return 20%.
- Variations: a single narrator answering a listener we never see.
- Fix: terminology burying the situation, or a metaphysical premise posed as science; keep it labelled fiction and leave room for disagreement.
- Finish test: does the idea change how the viewer sees an ordinary choice?

## STO6 · Social allegory / a visible rule for an invisible pressure
- Use when: one social pressure translates into a simple visual rule.
- Ingredients: the pressure as a rule of shape, colour, or rhythm; a recurring routine; a relationship that exposes the cost.
- Method: the routine working smoothly except for one human consequence → a small exception → pressure to conform → relational cost → a different choice → the routine replayed with an altered possibility. Stage: apply the rule consistently; give the exception room in the composition, not only brightness; an oppressive rhythm softens when the relationship changes.
- Timing: routine 25%, pressure and cost 45%, choice and replay 30%.
- Variations: a day-in-the-life loop that breaks on its last repetition.
- Fix: "all structure is bad"; let the metaphor sharpen a question, not settle it.
- Finish test: does the metaphor read through behaviour before anyone names it?

## STO7 · Rescue quest / adventure with a cost
- Use when: a protected relationship drives a journey that changes the hero.
- Ingredients: a bond; a loss or separation; skills shown before they are used; a destination that changes understanding.
- Method: urgent action or striking aftermath → the bond → loss → choice to pursue → obstacles needing different strategies → evidence complicating the mission → confrontation → outcome and its cost. Stage: keep travel direction and geography in action; alternate pursuit with a quiet memory; solve the climax with an established skill or object.
- Timing: bond and loss 25%, journey 50%, confrontation and cost 25%.
- Variations: vary obstacles across knowledge, sacrifice, trust, and physical challenge.
- Fix: spectacle without a reason to care; establish the bond before the lore.
- Finish test: does the outcome change the meaning of the original goal?

## STO8 · Mythic journey / seasonal transformation
- Use when: a small figure performs a ritual that transforms a landscape.
- Ingredients: a landscape with an expressive rule; a small protagonist; a ritual or task; a visible transformation.
- Method: a familiar seasonal state with one impossible sign → approach the threshold with purpose → learn the rule through an encounter → attempt the action → the environment transforms → return changed. Stage: scale links the small figure to the large world; weather, light, and musical phrases develop together; hold transformations long enough to be felt.
- Timing: landscape and approach 30%, rule and attempt 40%, transformation and return 30%.
- Variations: a journey across seasons or places, each stop transformed.
- Fix: explanatory dialogue or arbitrary lore; let the visual relationship carry meaning.
- Finish test: can the viewer feel the world change?

## Finish tests for the shelf

- Does the film still contain a meaningful change with its final speech removed?
- Can a shorter excerpt deliver one complete emotional beat without spoiling the whole?
