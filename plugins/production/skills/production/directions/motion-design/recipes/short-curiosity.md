# Short animation recipes: curiosity and open forms

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Curiosity, suspense and wonder (A5, A6, A8) and shorts without a conventional plot (A9, A10), for 3 to 10 minutes. Apply the shelf rules for length, combining and distinctiveness in [comedy and emotional change](directions/motion-design/recipes/short-comedy-emotion.md). Every timing is a starting allocation for the runtime it names.

## A5 · Fair Misunderstanding

- Use when: an empathy shift, a compact mystery, or a reveal that rewards rewatching. Runs 3 to 6 minutes.
- Ingredients: one limited viewpoint; behaviour with two plausible meanings; two truthful clues; one missing context; an action after the reveal.
- Method: write what is really happening before the misleading version → choose the viewpoint that makes a reasonable but incomplete reading likely → show evidence consistent with both readings, building the apparent conflict through actions rather than ominous music → reveal the missing relationship or context without replacing earlier facts → someone acts differently because of the discovery.
- Timing: for 4:00, 0:00–0:30 assumption; 0:30–1:20 first clue; 1:20–2:30 escalation and second clue; 2:30–3:15 reveal; 3:15–4:00 response.
- Variations: the misreading can be comic, affectionate or unsettling; viewer and protagonist need not learn the truth together.
- Fix: if the reveal feels like cheating, audit every earlier image against the real explanation and make at least one clue plainly visible.
- Finish test: On a second viewing, does the film get richer rather than inconsistent?

## A6 · Rule, Plan, Complication, Choice

- Use when: a caper, rescue, escape, puzzle or suspense short. Runs 5 to 10 minutes.
- Ingredients: one unusual rule with a limit; a concrete objective; a simple plan; a complication caused by the rule; a meaningful cost.
- Method: demonstrate the rule in a small event, no lecture → establish the goal and enough plan to anticipate progress → the rule creates trouble as well as opportunity and removes a convenient option → force a new choice: change the goal, accept a cost or use a known property differently → resolve through the known rule and show the end state so viewers can verify it.
- Timing: for 8:00, 0:00–0:55 rule; 0:55–1:45 plan; 1:45–4:20 execution; 4:20–5:45 complication; 5:45–7:15 choice and resolution; 7:15–8:00 aftermath.
- Variations: a deadline adds urgency, a competitor adds improvisation, a sacrifice adds emotional weight.
- Fix: if the mechanics become unreadable, remove one rule and show the geography before speeding up the action.
- Finish test: Can a viewer explain why the first plan failed and why the final action worked?

## A8 · Wonder with a Human Anchor

- Use when: discovery, belonging, coming of age, or an imaginative world reveal. Runs 5 to 10 minutes.
- Ingredients: an ordinary activity; one surprising world fact; a character who must take part; a relationship or dilemma; a practical discovery.
- Method: open on a familiar action for orientation → reveal one extraordinary world fact and hold the reaction long enough for wonder to land → give it a practical implication: a job, a convention to learn or conflicting approaches → the character finds a response that shows who they are → return to the larger spectacle with a newly human meaning.
- Timing: for 7:00, 0:00–0:50 ordinary anchor; 0:50–2:00 reveal; 2:00–3:30 participation; 3:30–5:15 dilemma; 5:15–6:15 discovery; 6:15–7:00 final image.
- Variations: the reveal grows in scale or deepens in meaning; prefer one coherent world to a tour of unrelated wonders.
- Fix: if scenery replaces story, ask what the discovery makes the character do next.
- Finish test: Does the spectacle mean more at the end because of the character's experience?

## A9 · A Small Victory in an Unfinished Life

- Use when: relatable adult stories, everyday or workplace comedy, gentle branded entertainment. Runs 3 to 6 minutes.
- Ingredients: one routine; one specific friction; a local response; a small gain or compromise; evidence that ordinary life continues.
- Method: enter the routine late, with something already happening → isolate one problem for this episode → a practical response reveals personality and priorities → complete the local action with a credible gain, compromise or affectionate failure → return to the routine with one relationship or behaviour changed.
- Timing: for 4:00, 0:00–0:35 routine; 0:35–1:20 friction; 1:20–2:50 response; 2:50–3:30 local result; 3:30–4:00 continuation.
- Variations: the reward may be a boundary, a shared laugh, one finished task or permission to leave something imperfect.
- Fix: if "life goes on" stands in for an ending, name the precise action that does finish.
- Finish test: Can someone describe the small change without inventing a grand transformation?

## A10 · Visual or Musical Transformation

- Use when: an abstract or musical short, identity film or concept with no protagonist that develops a contrast. For a deliberately stable cycle use [X4](directions/motion-design/recipes/essay.md); atmosphere-led visual music is [V2 or V7](directions/motion-design/recipes/music-video.md).
- Ingredients: one recognisable visual rule; a motif; controlled variation; a contradiction; a final relation that differs from the start.
- Method: teach the grammar through a simple repeated form, movement or phrase → vary one property while keeping the pattern recognisable → introduce a contrasting system related by collision, exchange, imitation, interruption or cooperation → let the encounter change how the original pattern behaves → end on a synthesis, separation or return that could not have meant the same at the start.
- Timing: for 3:00, 0:00–0:25 grammar; 0:25–1:00 variation; 1:00–1:45 contradiction; 1:45–2:35 development; 2:35–3:00 resolution. At 3 to 5 minutes use several developing movements rather than stretching one reveal.
- Variations: develop rhythm, density, order, colour relationships or positive and negative space.
- Fix: if the transformations feel interchangeable, state what each movement changes in the audience's expectation.
- Finish test: Can you describe a development rather than list materials and effects?
