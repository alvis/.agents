# Mystery and documentary recipes: real cases

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Make the evidence, question, or fictional rule visible. A mystery withholds the answer, never the information needed to understand the question.

## Evidence and fiction contract

- DOC1 to DOC4 make factual claims: each needs evidence from authoritative or direct sources, checked for current status; a reference film models format, never facts.
- Give records, reconstructions, illustrative models, and fiction distinct treatments, and draw observation differently from inference.
- End on supported findings plus remaining uncertainty; a narrower answer is a valid ending, a manufactured revelation is not.
- Never attribute intent, fabricate dialogue or quotations, accuse real people, sensationalise victims, or send viewers to unsafe sites, harassment, or exposing private individuals.
- Music follows the evidence: no cue may turn a possibility into an accusation or cast someone as hero or villain first.
- DOC5 to DOC8 are fiction: label them as fiction where they are presented, and never pass invented material off as an authentic record, warning, or recording.
- Routes: true crime uses DOC1 to DOC3; a geopolitical essay joins DOC2 to [HIS2](directions/motion-design/recipes/history-true.md); a civilisation autopsy joins DOC4 to checked historical evidence; fictional archives use DOC5.
- Timings are proportional starting allocations for a 60 s cut, refit to the bar grid; a long-form cut turns each stage into a chapter.

## DOC1 · Familiar thing with an unknown origin

- Use when: a recognisable object, symbol, or custom has a precise unanswered origin question.
- Ingredients: the thing, the question, competing explanations, a trail of evidence.
- Method: show the thing clearly, then the gap in its history → the precise question → a plausible explanation → evidence that narrows or overturns it → a stronger lead → supported conclusion and remaining uncertainty → back to the thing. Each document or comparison proves one point in close-up, never a wall of files.
- Timing: recognition 0–5 s, question 5–10 s, evidence 10–48 s, conclusion 48–56 s, return 56–60 s.
- Variations: a word, gesture, or brand mark works the same way.
- Fix: the earliest evidence found is called the first occurrence; say "earliest known" and show where the search stopped.
- Finish test: Can the viewer separate what is known from what remains unknown?

## DOC2 · Event reconstruction or failure chain

- Use when: a real event, engineering failure, or disaster has a reliable chronology and clear geography.
- Ingredients: the last well-understood state, the anomaly, a chronology, a map, competing explanations.
- Method: the last well-understood state and where the record turns anomalous → normal operation → the deviation → what can be established → explanations tested against the record → supported findings and unresolved parts. Align map, clock, and evidence; recorded positions differ visibly from inferred paths; silence sits around uncertainty.
- Timing: anomaly 0–6 s, normal operation 6–14 s, record 14–36 s, explanations 36–52 s, findings 52–60 s.
- Variations: a slow failure chain can replace a single event.
- Fix: an old documentary's conclusions are repeated as current; check authoritative sources for present status.
- Finish test: Can the viewer follow the chain and point to where the uncertainty sits?

## DOC3 · Internet mystery or puzzle trail

- Use when: a concrete digital artifact led to a trail of reproducible discoveries.
- Ingredients: the artifact, its demonstrable hidden layer, trail steps, widening community involvement, a defensible boundary to the answer.
- Method: an ordinary-looking image or message with a hidden layer → first reproducible discovery → a new destination or constraint → widening involvement → the major evidential turn → solved steps separated from speculation about creator or motive. Screens at readable scale; a breadcrumb trail shows where each clue came from; sonic cues mark real discoveries only.
- Timing: artifact 0–6 s, trail 6–44 s, turn 44–52 s, boundary 52–60 s.
- Variations: an alternate-reality game or viral hoax takes the same build.
- Fix: atmosphere replaces the chain; every step shows what was found and how.
- Finish test: Does the viewer understand the chain, not just the mood?

## DOC4 · Business rise, reinvention, or collapse

- Use when: a business model's advantage created its next problem.
- Ingredients: customer problem, the model, growth mechanism, hidden dependency, a decision under pressure, measured consequences.
- Method: apparent strength beside vulnerability, then one understandable transaction before any chart → customer problem → the model → growth → hidden dependency → decision under pressure → measured consequences → alternative explanations and a transferable lesson. Show the decision as a simple flow of customers, costs, or rights; keep observed metrics apart from illustrations.
- Timing: contrast 0–8 s, model and growth 8–28 s, dependency and decision 28–44 s, consequences 44–54 s, lesson 54–60 s.
- Variations: a reinvention ends on the new model instead of a decline.
- Fix: a hindsight morality play on a falling chart; verify quotations and financials in filings or direct sources, show the mechanism, and remember decline is not always collapse.
- Finish test: Can the viewer explain the mechanism without naming a hero or villain?

## Shelf finish test

Could a viewer tell which images are records, reconstructions, models, or fiction, and does each reveal change their understanding rather than add another mysterious noun?
