# Promotional reel recipes: desire, humour and identity

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

R4, R6, R8, R9 and R10. Choose the job and apply the shelf rules, duration table and proof rule in [explanation and proof](directions/motion-design/recipes/promo-proof.md) first. Every timing is a starting allocation for the runtime it names.

## R4 · Sensory Desire

- Use when: food, clothing, interiors, design objects or a tactile product. Default 10 s.
- Ingredients: one true desirable quality; an action that reveals it; a recognisable product; a satisfying finish.
- Method: open on the quality in use: a fold, break, pour, fit, opening or contact → develop it through one contrasting state or scale change → reveal or hold the actual product as the source of the pleasure → close with minimal copy and a usable invitation.
- Timing: for 10 s, 0–2 s sensory hook; 2–6 s development; 6–8 s result; 8–10 s identity. Make the product identifiable before the last two seconds.
- Variations: appetite, softness, precision, lightness or restraint; pick one. Luxury is not a synonym for a dark background.
- Fix: if the motion is generically beautiful, make the experience depend on a distinctive property of this product.
- Finish test: Could another product be swapped in without changing the film? If so, strengthen the connection.

## R6 · Familiar Frustration, Exaggerated

- Use when: relatable social comedy with an honest product connection. Default 15 s.
- Ingredients: a specific observed behaviour; one escalation rule; a punchline; a relevant brand role.
- Method: establish the recognisable behaviour fast → exaggerate the same nuisance in one coherent direction, never a random catastrophe → land a comic solution or an observation that changes how the audience sees it → connect the brand to that change and end.
- Timing: for 15 s, 0–3 s recognition; 3–7 s exaggeration; 7–11 s turn; 11–15 s brand and next action.
- Variations: make an invisible burden physical, personify an annoying process, or reverse who controls whom.
- Fix: if the joke is remembered but the offer is not, make the product's relevant action part of the punchline.
- Finish test: Does the audience recognise a behaviour, not just an exaggerated label about them?

## R8 · One Idea, Three Expressions

- Use when: a launch, identity, collection or campaign built on one organising idea. Default 20 s.
- Ingredients: one proposition; three distinct expressions of it; a visual invariant; a clear identity.
- Method: establish the recurring shape, action, relationship or phrase → express the proposition in one immediately readable situation → change context while keeping the invariant → let the third expression broaden or complete the idea, not add a message → resolve the invariant into the brand identity or final proposition.
- Timing: for 20 s, 0–2 s premise; 2–6 s first expression; 6–10 s second; 10–15 s third; 15–20 s identity and invitation.
- Variations: three users, settings or forms; never three unconnected product benefits. Three is a working count, not a rule.
- Fix: if it is a fashionable montage with no argument, write one sentence under every expression and check it truly fits each.
- Finish test: Are viewers left with one idea, or a memory of many effects?

## R9 · Invitation Completes the Pattern

- Use when: an event, class, community, workshop or participatory offer. Default 20 s.
- Ingredients: an appealing activity; a visible opening; an understandable contribution; a real next step.
- Method: show the experience in progress, not logistics text → make room to join: a role, a place, a missing response or a welcoming action → show what taking part looks like, at the demand level the intended audience can meet → name the offer and next action with time to read it, moving secondary details to the destination.
- Timing: for 20 s, 0–4 s activity; 4–9 s opening; 9–15 s participation; 15–20 s invitation.
- Variations: a missing musical response, a collaborative task or a place in a group; the gap must read as welcome, not exclusion.
- Fix: if it says "join us" without a clear experience, demonstrate what the viewer would actually do.
- Finish test: Can the real event deliver the invitation, including the experience level it implies?

## R10 · Mini-Song with a Visual Payoff

- Use when: a simple, repeatable proposition that gains from a sung phrase. Default 15 or 30 s.
- Ingredients: one short lyrical thought; a repeatable hook; a visible development; room for proof or comprehension; a clear finish.
- Method: write the plain message first and strip words that only praise the brand → turn it into a question and answer, contrast or short refrain on natural speech stresses → give the picture the situation and consequence while the lyric carries attitude → leave a wordless passage wherever viewers must inspect a demonstration → reprise the hook over the changed state and tie it to the brand.
- Timing: for 15 s on an optional 96 BPM grid (one 4/4 bar is 2.5 s, so sections land on bars), 0–5 s setup phrase; 5–7.5 s wordless action; 7.5–12.5 s answer; 12.5–15 s musical identity and hold. Fit lyrics to the grid with [music](directions/motion-design/music.md).
- Variations: call and response, an accumulating list, a rhythmic spoken line or a short sung refrain.
- Fix: if a feature list is forced into rhyme, keep one concrete image or useful promise and cut the rest.
- Finish test: Can a listener remember the phrase and a viewer understand the actual offer?
