# Music video recipes

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the track leads the piece.

Seven visual forms for animation built around a song or instrumental. The recording is the timing authority, so each Timing line names the unit taken from the track, not an allocation. A plot is optional; start from the music's structure, not a three-act arc laid over it.

## Shelf rules

- Pick by what the picture adds: performer presence V1, musical relationships V2, one sustained feeling V3, a returning section V4, the words V5, an added story V6, atmosphere or repeat listening V7.
- A hybrid needs one governing relationship, such as V1 performance anchoring a V4 refrain. Combining every form is not responding to the song.

## V1 · Animated Performance, Evolving Stage

- Use when: a vocal identity, virtual performer, dance-led piece, or band with character.
- Ingredients: a performing presence; signature gestures; a legible stage; one rule for how it changes.
- Method: name the performer's attitude in action, not a costume list → tie a gesture to a musical phrase → let the stage respond at chosen moments without replacing the performance → develop the gesture through partners, scale, resistance, or proximity → return to a recognisable performer image at the close or loop.
- Timing: phrases and sections; let gestures finish rather than cutting on every drum hit.
- Variations: solo, ensemble exchange, impossible choreography, performer dissolving into the imagery.
- Fix: if the performer becomes ornament in the scenery, give the key phrases a deliberate look or gesture.
- Finish test: Does a specific performing personality come through before the spectacle?

## V2 · Visual Score

- Use when: instrumental or rhythm-led music, abstract animation, a music-led identity.
- Ingredients: two or three musical behaviours; a visible counterpart for each; one shared space; planned interactions.
- Method: pick the roles worth seeing (pulse, sustained line, answer, texture, silence) → give each a distinct behaviour: one sets a field, one travels it, one interrupts it → repeat the mappings until viewers read the music through them → develop interactions as the music does → close on the music's resolution or cycle, with no added moral.
- Timing: phrase, section, then whole-track contour; local accents are seasoning.
- Variations: geometric, organic, typographic, or non-representational; mappings are choices, not universal meanings.
- Fix: if everything pulses to one beat, give each layer its own role or reduce to one relationship.
- Finish test: Can a viewer recognise musical relationships without a story to explain them?

## V3 · One Emotional Metaphor

- Use when: a song built on one sustained feeling such as longing, release, or intimacy.
- Ingredients: one emotional tension; one visual relationship; a small family of transformations; a chosen relation to the lyric.
- Method: state the tension as a relationship (reaching without arriving, holding while slipping) → choose a visual system that can express it repeatedly → introduce it plainly, then vary scale, texture, distance, or ownership → let each musical change show another facet of the same tension, not a new metaphor per noun → end in release, suspension, or return as the song does.
- Timing: the performance's emotional phrasing, which may cross bar or verse lines.
- Variations: the image echoes the lyric, names a feeling the words do not, or contradicts the singer.
- Fix: if the montage feels interchangeable, write the relation under each sequence and cut images that do not develop it.
- Finish test: Do the images belong to this song rather than a mood board?

## V4 · Verse Worlds, Visual Refrain

- Use when: music with returning sections, choruses or not.
- Ingredients: one visual refrain; distinct material for other sections; a rule for what changes on each return.
- Method: mark sections by listening, without assuming verse-chorus form → give the strongest recurring image to the recurring musical idea → use other sections for contrast or context → on each return keep recognition and change one property (participants, openness, direction, material, viewpoint) → decide whether the last return completes a development or celebrates repetition.
- Timing: section lengths from the track; a repeated chorus may repeat imagery without growing.
- Variations: one place with changing behaviour; one gesture passed between figures; dense and empty worlds alternating.
- Fix: if each section brings an unrelated look, set a recurring anchor or drop a competing world.
- Finish test: Is the return recognisable, and does its change have a musical reason?

## V5 · Lyric as Image

- Use when: a lyric video, language-led animation, or a track whose wordplay is the pleasure.
- Ingredients: the approved lyric; phrase timing; a readable type hierarchy; a few expressive behaviours.
- Method: split the lyric into meaningful phrases, not a word per screen → mark the words whose stress changes the thought, following the singer → show relationships by spacing, grouping, repetition, or replacement → hold complex phrases still long enough to read → give the refrain one distinctive transformation.
- Timing: sung phrases plus reading time; a held vowel can carry a visual hold.
- Variations: full lyric, key phrases only, or typographic performance; key phrases do not replace captions where access needs them.
- Fix: if words are on screen but unreadable, remove competing motion, regroup by phrase, and lengthen the hold.
- Finish test: Can a viewer follow the wording and feel why some words are stressed?

## V6 · Parallel Narrative

- Use when: a song that gains depth from an added sequence of events.
- Ingredients: one compact story; its emotional relation to the track; a few turns; room for performance or instrumental passages.
- Method: state what the song expresses and what the story adds, related but not literal → build the causal chain separately, with a [story device](references/story-devices.md) where useful → place major turns on meaningful musical changes → move action that would compete with key lyrics into instrumental spans → decide whether story and song converge, complicate, or stay in tension.
- Timing: the fixed track length; simplify the story until it fits.
- Variations: a remembered event, a contrasting outer life, an unexpected viewpoint, intercut performance.
- Fix: if the song becomes wallpaper, rework the story's relation to it or choose another form.
- Finish test: Would swapping the track change the interpretation?

## V7 · A Place to Inhabit

- Use when: a visualiser, ambient release, repeat-listening or mood-led piece.
- Ingredients: a coherent world; a focal anchor; movement on several timescales; optional section-tied changes; a deliberate loop or exit.
- Method: decide what spending time here feels like → give an anchor and a few recurring behaviours → separate frequent movement from rare change → let chosen musical changes alter density, light, or presence without breaking the world → end in a smooth return, sustained state, or quiet exit.
- Timing: the listening experience; a long piece needs subtle variation, not an event every few seconds.
- Variations: a still scene with small motion, slow drift, or detail revealed over time.
- Fix: if a calm brief turns restless, reduce salient changes and keep the anchor; if it is dull, make the world more specific, not the cuts faster.
- Finish test: Does repeat viewing support the music without a jarring reset or a promised reveal that never comes?
