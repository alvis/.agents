# Science and discovery recipes: mechanisms

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

Give the viewer something to predict, inspect, or operate, not just something to be told. The [explainer shelf](directions/motion-design/recipes/explainer-mechanism.md)'s accuracy rules apply here in full.

## Shelf rules

- Verify explanations, models, numbers, and practical advice in the subject's own sources; a reference film models format, never facts.
- Label speculation, approximations, and model limits on screen.
- The topic is not the format: one subject can become a what-if, cutaway, experiment, or puzzle, each with a different opening promise.
- Route myth-busting to E4, practical demonstrations to E5, and data stories, rankings, or comparisons to E7 in the [explainer practice shelf](directions/motion-design/recipes/explainer-practice.md); use E8 only when a character's decision depends on the lesson.
- Timings are proportional starting allocations for a 60 s cut, refit to the bar grid.

## DIS1 · What-if consequence ladder

- Use when: one unusual change to a recognisable system can reveal a real dependency.
- Ingredients: the single change, stated assumptions, consequences at several scales, the model's limit.
- Method: a familiar scene with one condition altered, baseline visible long enough to compare → define the change → immediate consequence → less obvious secondary effect → larger-system consequence → limit of the model → what this reveals about the ordinary world. Widen the shot as causal reach grows, keeping the original object as scale anchor; drop the score under a hard mechanism.
- Timing: altered scene 0–5 s, three rungs 5–45 s, limit 45–52 s, return 52–60 s.
- Variations: a changed historical condition belongs to [HIS8](directions/motion-design/recipes/history-everyday.md).
- Fix: spectacle strung together, or speculation presented as prediction; state why each rung follows from the last.
- Finish test: Does the silly question now reveal a real dependency?

## DIS2 · Visual mathematical discovery

- Use when: a concrete puzzle or construction contains a general relationship the viewer can feel being discovered.
- Ingredients: the construction, one element to manipulate, an invariant or emerging pattern, a fresh test case.
- Method: the construction on screen before any terminology → the concrete question → manipulate one element → notice the invariant → express it generally → test a changed case → say where the reasoning applies. Objects keep their identity as they become diagram elements or symbols; move one relationship at a time; hold quietly while the viewer infers.
- Timing: construction 0–8 s, manipulation and pattern 8–35 s, generalisation 35–45 s, new case 45–55 s, scope 55–60 s.
- Variations: let the viewer attempt one small inference before the reveal.
- Fix: a beautiful morph stands in for a proof; disclose approximations and limiting steps, and bring in symbols only after the picture has earned them.
- Finish test: Could the viewer rediscover the result from the picture?

## DIS3 · Hidden system behind a familiar frustration

- Use when: a relatable nuisance such as a phantom traffic jam, a queue, or a bottleneck has an invisible mechanism.
- Ingredients: the experience, one unit to follow, its effect on the next, the system pattern, one changeable condition.
- Method: the visible symptom, then pull back to the missing relationship → isolate one unit → trace its effect on the next → the system pattern → change one condition → the result and its practical limits. One route and orientation; highlight only the followed unit; repeated motion shows propagation; a recurring click can make delay audible.
- Timing: symptom 0–6 s, propagation 6–30 s, pattern 30–40 s, changed condition 40–54 s, limits 54–60 s.
- Variations: deliveries and information networks take the same build.
- Fix: the illustrative model is sold as a complete policy solution; say what real cases add.
- Finish test: Can the viewer explain the hidden process next time they meet the nuisance?

## DIS4 · Engineering cutaway

- Use when: a familiar machine hides an internal relation whose parts can be demonstrated.
- Ingredients: normal use, the internal question, parts with demonstrable roles, one safety or control feature.
- Method: normal use, then the casing comes off to show the part doing the work → external-to-internal handover → one motion or force → coupled parts → the safety or control feature → reassemble and run the whole cycle. Hold one orientation; every arrow has a named meaning; a small sound marks each handover.
- Timing: normal use 0–5 s, handovers 5–40 s, safety feature 40–50 s, full cycle 50–60 s.
- Variations: [Blender](directions/motion-design/blender.md) suits an exploded view beyond web animation.
- Fix: invisible or invented components make the animation work; model only real parts. It is not a maintenance or emergency manual.
- Finish test: Can the viewer trace how input becomes output?

## Shelf finish test

Pause before the reveal: does the audience know what to look for? After it, can they explain the result without repeating the narrator's last line?
