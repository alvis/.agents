# Comedy and internet formats

Read from `directions/motion-design/concept.md` when the chosen recipe is on this shelf.

## Comic timing

- A comic hook makes an expectation legible; the joke changes it in a way that suddenly makes sense. Before animating, write the expectation, the reversal, and why they connect; sketch only setup, action, and reaction.
- The viewer reads the setup before the surprise arrives, and the piece stops once the joke lands. Cut any line that only explains a visually unclear joke.
- A short carries one complete comic mechanism. A longer episode alternates mechanisms (recognition, escalation, wordplay, visual reveal, callback) instead of repeating one twist.
- Hold the reaction after a result, with a breath or blink so the hold reads as a beat, not the stall [build](directions/motion-design/build.md) warns against.
- Sound test: play the scene without music, then without dialogue, and name the channel carrying the joke. An impact sound or laugh track cannot repair a missing premise.
- Timings are starting allocations.

## COM1 · Animated personal storytime
- Use when: a narrator recounts one specific lived experience with a recognisable frustration.
- Ingredients: one incident; the narrator's flawed expectation; their attempted response; a revised opinion; a real narrator telling facts they gave, or an explicitly fictional one ([motion-design](directions/motion-design.md) step 2 owns the subject rule).
- Method: open inside the awkward moment, before background → confession or opinion → the incident → attempted response → escalating misunderstanding → consequence → self-aware callback. Stage: alternate narrator-to-viewer with short reenactments; show the feeling exaggerated, then what actually happened; a pause beats a loud effect.
- Timing: 60–90 s: hook under 5 s, incident and escalation 60%, consequence and callback the last 20%.
- Variations: a chain of incidents escalating the same misunderstanding.
- Fix: a list of generic grievances; anchor on one incident and escalate it.
- Finish test: does the ending revise the opinion the narrator opened with?

## COM2 · Wordless observational comedy
- Use when: the humour is precise everyday behaviour in a familiar relationship, without dialogue.
- Ingredients: two characters with conflicting immediate needs (attention against rest); three genuinely different tactics; a closing behavioural detail.
- Method: both needs readable in one wide shot → polite tactic → clever tactic → overcommitted disastrous tactic → consequence or reversal → behavioural detail. Stage: stable geography so the viewer anticipates; breathing, small movements, and one unexpected sound replace dialogue.
- Timing: 30–60 s: mismatch 15%, each tactic about 20%, reversal and detail the rest.
- Variations: housemates, colleagues, an appliance, or a personified object.
- Fix: the same action repeated louder; escalate the strategy instead.
- Finish test: is each tactic smarter or more desperate than the last?

## COM3 · Absurd micro-sketch / literalisation
- Use when: an ordinary phrase or convention can be taken literally or broken by category.
- Ingredients: an ordinary exchange; an alternative interpretation; its immediate consequence; a completing reaction.
- Method: start mid-exchange → a gap for the viewer to predict → literal or category-breaking response → reaction → cut. Stage: simple set, strong silhouettes, still camera, silence between line and reveal.
- Timing: 5–10 s: setup about half, then the gap, reversal, and reaction.
- Variations: a compilation of original premises, each reset clearly.
- Fix: randomness with no readable expectation; plant the expected reading first.
- Finish test: is the surprise connected to the setup?

## COM4 · Social satire / a system made ridiculous
- Use when: one recognisable incentive or habit defeats its own stated purpose.
- Ingredients: the promise; the rule people follow; a literal visual metaphor; a compounding contradiction; one focal character or object.
- Method: people diligently doing a task that defeats its purpose → the promise → the rule → its hidden cost → the same rule producing a larger absurdity → changed viewpoint or deliberately unresolved contradiction. Stage: repetition, scale, and crowds make pressure tangible; cheerful institutional sound over worsening images creates irony.
- Timing: 1–3 min: promise and rule 25%, cost and escalation 60%, ending 15%.
- Variations: one character's day under the system, replayed with rising cost.
- Fix: "everything is terrible" is too broad; name one mechanism, and aim at incentives and power, not stereotypes.
- Finish test: can the viewer name the mechanism afterwards?

## COM5 · Parody / the sensible alternative ending
- Use when: a genre convention ignores an obvious practical option.
- Ingredients: the convention; the neglected option; a consequence that comments on the genre.
- Method: just enough of the dilemma, in the genre's serious style → sensible interruption that breaks its rhythm → new choice → different outcome → second consequence commenting on the genre. Stage: original staging readable by viewers who know only the broad convention.
- Timing: 30–90 s: setup 30%, interruption and outcome 50%, comment 20%.
- Variations: several conventions from one genre, each with its sensible fix.
- Fix: a copied scene with an unrelated punchline; make the second consequence critique the source. Inspiration grants no right to footage, music, characters, or designs; record rights through [track](directions/track.md).
- Finish test: does the joke say something about the convention?

## COM6 · Game-world / sandbox adventure
- Use when: a character discovers a world with legible rules and plays with them.
- Ingredients: clear world rules; a playful discovery; a small objective; combinable abilities; original characters and assets.
- Method: show the new power once, cleanly, before explaining → experiment → small success → misuse or combine abilities → a consequence the rules create → solution through a capability already shown. Stage: wide readable shots; one visual and sound cue per mechanic, reused every time.
- Timing: 1–3 min: discovery 15%, play 50%, consequence and solution 35%.
- Variations: two characters forced to combine different abilities.
- Fix: a final power never shown before; plant it earlier.
- Finish test: is the solution earned from rules the viewer saw?

## COM7 · Interface / screenlife comedy
- Use when: a familiar interface can become an obstacle or weapon.
- Ingredients: a recognisable screen; an actor with a clear goal; one rule to subvert; an overlooked established affordance.
- Method: normal rule → one violation → attempted correction → the interface's counter-move → escalation through existing controls → resolution through the overlooked affordance. Stage: one active screen region at a time; cursor travel, typing indicators, and notification delays as anticipation; text readable without replaying.
- Timing: 30–90 s: normal rule under 10%, escalation 70%, resolution the rest.
- Variations: chat screenlife, with messages and typing states as the action.
- Fix: a fake screen that could pass as genuine; keep it visibly fictional, never real evidence, a real person's messages, or a security demonstration.
- Finish test: does the ending use a control the viewer already saw?

## COM8 · Object show / elimination competition
- Use when: a cast of objects or simple characters competes in an episodic challenge.
- Ingredients: a small cast with clear contrasts; a visible challenge; understandable stakes; a recurring arena or scoreboard.
- Method: challenge introduced through a personality collision, prize beside the first disagreement → define the round → temporary alliances → personalities shape tactics → actions change the challenge → result → one relational question for next episode. Stage: group characters by action; few sound motifs; never the whole cast talking at once.
- Timing: 3–8 min episode: setup 15%, challenge 65%, result and hook 20%.
- Variations: a one-round short that resolves fully.
- Fix: more contestants than roles; cut the cast until each earns one. Never imply audience votes decide an outcome without a real vote.
- Finish test: does the episode resolve something while the competition continues?
