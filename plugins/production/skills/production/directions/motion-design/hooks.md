# Hooks and openings

Read from `directions/motion-design.md` when writing the storyboard's opening, payoff and catchphrases.

Choose one main viewing reward (a laugh, a feeling, understanding, a satisfying process, immersion) before drafting; an opening that tries to surprise, move, teach and amuse at once promises nothing. These are planning tools, not predictions of platform reach. The payoff, share reason, catchphrases, replay and packaging live in [payoffs](directions/motion-design/payoffs.md).

## Four passes

- **Entry**: why this audience starts: a recognisable desire, problem, contradiction or promised experience.
- **Continuation**: why they stay: consequences develop, understanding builds, a phrase returns, or a state is sustained. Not every form needs rising stakes.
- **Reward**: what is delivered: a laugh, an emotional change, an explanation, a transformation, evidence.
- **Transmission**: what a viewer can say when sending it on.

## Opening recipes

A hook need not ask "what happens next?". It can ask "why does this work?", "how do I do it?", "how will this phrase return?", or simply invite the viewer to stay in a sound and image.

| Code | Opening | Question it opens | Payoff owed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 · Recognisable friction | A specific familiar task already obstructed by a recognisable nuisance | How do they get past this? | A useful solution or a precise joke about the frustration |
| H2 · One impossible detail | An ordinary situation where exactly one part behaves impossibly | Why is that happening? | A readable rule, consequence or reveal |
| H3 · Imminent result | The moment just before a meaningful consequence: the last piece, a test, a precarious choice | Will it work? | The actual result, not another teaser |
| H4 · Result first | An intriguing finished state | How did that happen? | A credible causal route or a demonstration |
| H5 · Small goal, visible barrier | Someone wanting something simple, beside the obstacle | How can they get it? | An attempt worth following and a changed outcome |
| H6 · Social recognition | A precise behaviour from a situation the audience knows | Do they get this like I do? | A specific observation, not a stereotype |
| H7 · Satisfying process | A visible rule, progress, one meaningful incompletion | What will the finished pattern look like? | Completion plus an insight, use or elegant surprise |
| H8 · Musical question | A short phrase or rhythm that leaves an identifiable gap | What answers that? | A musical and visual answer, not endless withholding |
| H9 · Useful question | A specific question or comparison the learner wants answered, with the minimum conditions | How does it work? | An accurate mechanism or usable skill, not merely a curious fact |
| H10 · Expressive identity | A distinctive sound–image relation: a gesture, visual phrase, lyric arrangement or atmosphere | None needed | Immersion and recurrence |

Choose by brief: a product viewers do not understand, H1 or H4; a visually obvious capability, H3; comedy, H5 or H6; wonder, H2; a tactile or abstract identity piece, H7 or H8; a lesson, H9; a music- or mood-led piece, H10, without manufacturing a mystery to imitate a narrative hook.

Method: draft three genuinely different openings (for example scale contrast, a direct question, a character caught inside the event), each honest to the central claim → keep the one that most clearly promises the payoff the film delivers.

## Stage the hook as a scene

Write each opening as what the viewer sees, what they hear, what they now wonder or enjoy, and what the next beat delivers. A striking line over unrelated animation is not a hook; the picture must add information the words lack.

Method: title and cover → first moving image → first meaningful line or sound → first local reward → next justified development, all one continuous promise.

Opening compositions, chosen by the development they promise:

- **Small cause, enormous response**: a tiny object in the foreground, a disproportionate reaction behind it; return to the object once the real cause is clear.
- **Expectation beside reality**: two comparable states in one frame with one decisive difference highlighted; then test explanations.
- **Outside to inside**: ordinary use, then the casing comes off with orientation and the action's sound kept; then trace the mechanism.
- **Controlled prediction**: goal and constraint held long enough to predict; then a fair result without changing the rules.
- **Desire and obstacle in one shot**: the wanted object, the character and the obstacle spatially legible; then attempts that each change the situation.
- **Clue with a gap**: a familiar symbol, document or screen fills the frame with one thing nobody can explain; then an evidence trail with local answers.
- **Routine with one wrong detail**: a credible ordinary composition in which one anomalous instruction, space or sound emerges; the anomaly must change how the world works.
- **Immediate sound–image pleasure**: a musical impact, tactile transformation, pose or inviting place arrives with its matching sound; then variation, completion or a sustained state.

Readability: one focal relationship before detail ([C1](directions/motion-design/composition.md)); captions clear of player controls at the target aspect; a joke's setup registers before the reversal; a music-led opening is judged against the actual phrase, not a silent board.

Timing, as starting allocations:

- 10–30 s reel: the opening situation is legible within the first 1–3 s; a quiet image can hook through an interesting relation.
- 30–60 s piece: premise in the first few seconds, then the minimum context or rule, two or three beats, and protected time for the answer.
- 3–10 min: the opening already holds a relevant observation, tension or pleasure; the first small answer comes before background; each chapter answers one question and opens the next.
- Music video or ambient: the track's phrasing is the timing authority; never cut away from the strongest musical moment to meet a hook rule.

Finish test: does the opening promise the film that follows, or a more sensational one that was never made?

## Keep the middle developing

Method: set an expectation → give a partial reward → change one consequential variable → make the next outcome worth anticipating.

- **Progress with a cost**: the attempt itself creates the new problem; random complications do not count.
- **Escalating strategy**: the next attempt uses a different idea, not more force or a restyled repeat of the same failure.
- **Information shift**: a clue changes the interpretation; never withhold basic geography or rules.
- **Relationship shift**: someone changes side, asks for help or reveals a preference through behaviour, not a speech.
- **Rhythmic contrast**: a revealing pause after action, or decisive action after a held question; not another cut or sound effect.

Give every part a job: explanation, practice, new consequence, new relationship, musical phrase, association, satisfying cycle or deliberate hold. A repeated chorus or steady atmosphere with a job stays.
