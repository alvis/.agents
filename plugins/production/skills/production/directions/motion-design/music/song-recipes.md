# Song recipes

Read from `directions/motion-design/music.md` when writing lyrics, a hook, or the musical material a composition plan carries.

Musical recipes M1–M8. Write their words and melody with the craft in [songwriting](directions/motion-design/music/songwriting.md).

## Recipes

M1–M4 instrumental expression, M5 micro-jingle, M6 narrative song, M7 teaching song, M8 narration-led score. To fit phrases into a fixed slot, use [scoring](directions/motion-design/music/scoring.md).

## M1 · Motif, Variation, Changed Return

- Use when: a character's development or a relationship with recurring gestures.
- Ingredients: a motif recognisable unaccompanied; the person or action it belongs to; one property to keep, one to change.
- Method: write it on a few notes → introduce it on its defining action → change its instrument, register, harmony, or rhythm as the story moves → return with the earned change.
- Timing: no allocation; the introduction and final return are the placements that matter.
- Variations: a question with a rest another voice later answers.
- Fix: if each return only gets louder, change ownership, phrasing, or harmonic destination.
- Finish test: Does the return mean something it did not at first?

## M2 · Pattern, Anticipation, Comic Interruption

- Use when: repeated attempts, overconfidence, or a comic reveal.
- Ingredients: a repeatable phrase; an expected accent; a timed deviation; a reaction.
- Method: establish the phrase on the first attempt → repeat until its ending is predictable → delay, remove, or swap the accent for an ordinary sound → leave room for the reaction.
- Timing: two repetitions set the pattern before the break.
- Variations: two proud flourishes, then a small real-world sound.
- Fix: if every movement gets a hit, support the intention and save exact sync for the joke.
- Finish test: Does the sound improve the timing rather than announce the joke?

## M3 · Pulse, Pressure, Decision, Release

- Use when: a chase, test, rescue, deadline, or suspenseful reveal.
- Ingredients: a clear pulse; an unresolved question; one rising pressure; a place to stop or turn.
- Method: set the pulse → raise one pressure (density, register, subdivision, instability), never starting at maximum → remove or redirect the pulse at the choice → resolve, stay unsettled, or stop as the outcome dictates.
- Timing: no allocation; the decision is the must-hit.
- Variations: shrinking gaps at steady tempo; silence under the irreversible gesture.
- Fix: if the cue just keeps rising, find the real change of stakes and restructure around it.
- Finish test: Is the climax a change of meaning, not only volume?

## M4 · Product Sound, Rhythm, Musical Identity

- Use when: a tactile product, process reel, or sound-led identity.
- Ingredients: a characteristic real sound; a simple rhythm; a contrasting texture; a short ending gesture.
- Method: tie the sound to the visible action → repeat or answer it into a phrase → add only needed accompaniment → resolve into an identity gesture.
- Timing: the ending gesture lands while the product is visible.
- Variations: a closure or contact sound as the beat; a held tone only after the result.
- Fix: if generic swishes replace the product, return to the sound it actually makes.
- Finish test: Would the cue suit an unrelated product equally well?

## M5 · Micro-Jingle: One Thought, One Hook

- Use when: a 10–30 s reel with one proposition.
- Ingredients: one plain message; a memorable phrase; its natural stresses; a simple contour; space to read and breathe.
- Method: say the message as a person would → build a rhythmic phrase on the meaning words → hum a melody serving those stresses → repeat the hook once with a changed image, leaving a wordless span → tie it to the brand and finish inside the slot, decay included.
- Timing: for 15 s, the sung-cue allocation in [scoring](directions/motion-design/music/scoring.md).
- Variations: frustration, answer, phrase; or question, answer, refrain.
- Fix: if the melody forces unnatural speech, shorten or rewrite the line.
- Finish test: Can someone recall the phrase and link it to the right offer?

## M6 · Narrative Song with a Changed Refrain

- Use when: a musical short or a repeated situation that changes meaning.
- Ingredients: one speaker; one refrain; advancing verses; a turning point; a changed final reading.
- Method: put the opening belief in the refrain → each verse adds an event, not synonyms → at the turn change a key word, speaker, or harmony, keeping the refrain recognisable → let the final image carry what the lyric leaves unsaid.
- Timing: belief, consequence, refrain, consequence, changed understanding, final refrain, with instrumental space for action.
- Variations: one changed pronoun, "I am still here" becoming "we are still here", once the verses earn it.
- Fix: if the song explains what is visible, give the lyric the inner view and the picture the action.
- Finish test: Does the last refrain mean something new without a closing explanation?

## M7 · Teaching Song: Recall with Meaning

- Use when: an accurate sequence, distinction, or rule worth a sung refrain.
- Ingredients: one objective; a fact-checked statement with its conditions; a refrain; visual examples; a gap for the learner.
- Method: write the concept in prose with conditions → set the relation as a refrain, rewriting rhyme before fact → demonstrate it in examples with repeated visual anchors → leave a gap to predict, then explain → end on a new example.
- Timing: starting allocation for 2:00: 0:00–0:15 question; 0:15–0:35 example; 0:35–0:50 refrain; 0:50–1:15 contrasting example; 1:15–1:35 learner response; 1:35–2:00 explanation and refrain.
- Variations: cumulative sequence, two confusable terms, call and response, a causal chain.
- Fix: if catchy but inaccurate, narrow the line and show its limit; if recalled but not applied, add a fresh, less guided example.
- Finish test: Can the learner use the idea correctly without the melody?

## M8 · Narration Leads, Music Makes Room

- Use when: explainers, documentaries, instructions, evidence-led animation.
- Ingredients: paced narration; its section map; a restrained identity; deliberate unscored spans.
- Method: time the narration and mark dense reasoning → give the music one job (orientation, continuity, curiosity) → thin or stop it around the hardest sentences → mark only real changes of question → release on the shown result, claiming no more certainty than the evidence.
- Timing: identity, restrained support, space, small bridge, conclusion; silence is valid anywhere.
- Variations: a sound per stage; a motif for the main question; no score under a worked calculation.
- Fix: if exciting but hard to follow, remove musical information before cutting explanation.
- Finish test: Does the score aid orientation without obscuring words or evidence?
